# Known functions: car physics

The cars' physics and the collision world they move in. The car data
record (vehicle +272) is filled from the car's spec (asset 72, through the
car object's copies; `car-specs.md`) and derived once per car load
(`820AECB8`-`820AF1C0`, from the car loader `Vehicle::SetupFromCar`,
`sub_820C27E8`); the
vehicle's physics set-up (`sub_820B5670`, `sub_820B5B08`) makes its rigid
body, its collision object and the object at +344; the physics step
(`Vehicle::PhysicsStep`, `sub_820B6498`, probably once a tick) works out
the ground under each wheel, the suspension, steering, tires (Pacejka's
magic formula, `sub_820B09C8`), engine, turbo, gearbox, drivetrain,
brakes and aerodynamics, in 10 substeps where it counts, and integrates
the rigid body (`820B6A38`-`820B7A00`). The collision world at
`[0x826C2D88]` (`820B7AE8`-`820B9570`, `820BBC40`-`820BC068`) keeps 50
collision objects, their pairs (made and removed as objects' groups
change) and the contacts, and its update (`CollisionWorld::Update`,
`sub_820B92C8`, probably once a tick) collides objects with the track's
queries and responds with forces using the surface settings
(`820BC1F8`-`820BC550`: four values per surface kind, by flag bits
0x01000000, 0x02000000 and 0x04000000). The pairs' own narrow phase and
the collision shapes (`820B99D0`-`820BBE30`, `820BC848` on) are in
`collision.md`; the world's owner (its load, tick, reset and unload)
and the track's queries it calls are in `collision-world.md`.

Shared with other files, which keep their rows: the accessors on the
vehicle, its wheels, rigid body, collision object, contacts and the
collision world, and the vector library (`vehicle-accessors.md`); the
matrix and quaternion math (`matrix-math.md`); `sin`, `cos`, `tan`,
`atan`, `atan2`, `pow`, `memset`, `memcpy` (`c-runtime.md`). The heap
(`sub_825A6880`, probably operator new; `sub_825A6940`, `sub_825A71C0`,
`sub_825A70E8`) is a lead in `docs/loops/leads.md`: functions here that
call it allocate or free guest memory. `sub_820B0E10` (`SinCos`) is in
`matrix-math.md`, `sub_820AE958` stays in `functions.md`.

Floats named by address are in `.rdata`: `0x82000A30` is 1.0,
`0x82000A34` 0.0, `0x82001BE4` 0.5, `0x820029EC` 1/30; `0x82001BE0`
probably -1.0, `0x820021E8` probably 2.0, `0x82002968` probably 0.25;
`0x82953790` is probably the zero vector, `0x826381B0` the identity
quaternion and `0x82638270` an axis (probably x). Wheels 0 and 1 are the
front axle, 2 and 3 the rear. "Wheel +N" is in the wheel's 368-byte
record (vehicle +416 + 368 × wheel), "wheel data" the axle record at
`[vehicle + 2160 + 4 × wheel]` (car data +336 front, +608 rear) and
"wheel record 2" the axle's 128-byte record at `[vehicle + 2144 + 4 × wheel]`
(vehicle +1888 front, +2016 rear). The fields are in `docs/known-data.md`
(the vehicle's physics, the car data, the axle record, the rigid body,
the collision world, objects, pairs, hits and contacts, the surface
settings).

Things a native version has to keep (the game's own behavior): the step
relies on many callees leaving r3 (the vehicle) as it was (listed in
`sub_820B6498`'s row); `sub_820AFE90` normalizes a zero vector;
`sub_820B72C8` reads stack garbage for a fixed body; `sub_820B4778`
loads an unset stack slot it doesn't use on the one-axle paths;
`sub_820B4CF0` writes into the car data; surface group D's getter falls
back to group B's default.

Native so far (B7 loop, 2026-10-22, `--native_car_physics`, on by
default): the rigid body (`src/car_physics/rigid_body.cpp`) and the
physics step's helpers and the controls' setters
(`src/car_physics/vehicle_step.cpp`), adapters in
`src/hooks/car_physics.cpp`, harness `car_physics`. What it took: the
originals' temporaries are written in their own stack frames at the same
offsets (`frame`, the original's r1 after its `stwu`; callees' frames below
it), so what's left on the stack is the same; the PowerPC branches kept as
they are (`bge` and `ble` are "not less" and "not greater": NaNs take
them); an `lfs` then `stfs` of one word is a bit copy where they're next to
each other in the translated code, but goes through the double (a
signaling NaN made quiet) where a branch joins them (`sub_820AFD30`); the
vector library's dot product kept inside its flush-to-zero window (ThinLTO
had moved it out; see `sub_82294170` in `vehicle-accessors.md`). The
harness runs the vehicle's functions on a vehicle, its car data, surface
records and body 64 KB down the guest stack (as the sprites' harness), its
numbers below 10^6 so no NaN is made from infinities; where NaNs meet, the
cases are in "(expected to differ)" groups. Frames dumped 10 s into an
attract race show the AI cars in the same places with the flag on and off.

The second migration (B7 loop, 2026-10-22) made the physics step's updates
native (`src/car_physics/vehicle_update.cpp`, the same flag and harness):
the traction control, tire grip, engine torque, brake heat, backfire, tire
wear, steering, torque split, turbo, tire forces, the at-rest check, the
aerodynamics and the wheels' axes, and `Matrix::CopyRows` and
`MultiplyTranslation`. Callees made native earlier are called directly,
and their adapters removed where no recompiled caller is left
(SurfaceValue20, 12 and 16, `Tire::MagicFormula`). What it took, beyond the
first's: a register kept from one loop pass to the next is kept so
(`sub_820B35C8`'s heat added); integers converted through the stack (`std`
then `lfd`) are stored there as the original does; where a callee that
never ran in play or a C runtime error path would be taken (`sub_820B2100`'s
mode 2, tan's `_except1` in `sub_820B18B8`), the plain C++ decides that
first, with nothing done, and the adapter runs the original; r3 is
returned where the original's last call leaves something there (the
harness compares it). Read the `lis`/offset pairs' sums carefully: the
floats at `0x820029F4` to `0x820029FC` are next to `0x82002A34` to
`0x82002A3C`, and mixing them up was the one mistake the harness caught.

The third migration (B7 loop, 2026-10-22) made the rest of the physics
step's updates native but its root (`src/car_physics/vehicle_state.cpp`:
`SyncFromBody`, the suspension, the drivetrain and its differential, the
gearbox, the reset, gearing and steering, the wheel copies) and the
collision world's objects, contacts and surface settings
(`src/car_physics/collision_world.cpp`), the same flag and harness (the
collision world's cases in guest stack below the vehicle's, with the
surface settings and the world's pointer put back after). What it took,
beyond the first two's: which registers a callee leaves alone matters
where the original keeps values in volatile ones across a call
(`UpdateDrivetrain`, `SetUpGearing`: read from the callees' code); a
function whose temporaries are below r1 without a frame of its own
(`UpdateDrivenAxle`) has them written there; the gearbox's control flow
(549 lines) is written label for label with its registers as locals. A
deliberately broken line in the gearbox and in `CollisionRecords::Merge`
made their harness cases differ (their deep paths are reached).

The fourth migration (B7 loop, 2026-10-22) made the collision world's
pairs (added, linked, unlinked, moved, removed) and its reset native
(`src/car_physics/collision_world.cpp`, the same flag and harness: the
pairs' cases on a fake world of 2 to 8 objects in guest stack below the
collision cases', with pairs the recompiled `AddPairs` made first, the
same in both runs). The pairs are integers only; the one memcpy (a moved
pair) is `c_runtime::Memcpy`, checked before anything is done as the
vehicle accessors' copies are, the original running where it can't be. It also
made the car data's set-up native (`src/car_physics/car_data.cpp`: the
constructor and defaults, `FillAxle`, `ConvertUnits`) and the vehicle's
constructor, with the vehicle's harness cases. Then the objects'
removal and their groups (`RemoveObject`, `RemovePairsOf`,
`RemovePairsByGroups`, and `CollisionObject::AddGroups` and `RemoveGroups`
in `vehicle-accessors.md`): where one pair's removal can't be native, the
rest runs on the original from the start, which does the same as it would
have (removals don't change what it tests on the pairs before). A
deliberately broken line in each removal loop made their cases differ.

Status:

- **Native**: plain C++ in `itc_recomp/src/` that doesn't use the
  PowerPC context (`ctx`): normal arguments and host structs (it may
  still read and write guest memory through `src/guest.h` while
  recompiled code shares the data). The game reaches it without `ctx`,
  from native or overridden callers, and the recompiled original is no
  longer called (except behind a fallback flag or to check against it).
  No `REX_FUNC` override of it exists, not even an adapter kept only for
  a harness (that's Overridden until the adapter goes).
  The end state for every function.
- **Overridden**: an `extern "C" REX_FUNC(sub_XXXXXXXX)` override in
  `itc_recomp/src/` (or a macro defining one) does the work instead of
  the original: a thin adapter in front of native code, or a body that
  still uses `ctx` (calling guest code, reading registers). It may call
  the original when its flag is off, or to check itself against it.
- **Wrapped**: an override that does something of its own (skipping
  render-only passes, logging, timing) and still calls the original
  (`__imp__sub_XXXXXXXX`) for the work.
- **Dropped**: its work isn't needed on PC (hardware set-up, kernel calls
  the SDK already stubbed), so it no longer runs: an override in
  `itc_recomp/src/` replaces it with nothing (or a fixed return value), or
  its callers, made native, leave the call out (no override then; the row
  says which caller). Logs, a harness or the renderer's frame dumps have
  shown that nothing reads what it did. The original is no longer called,
  except behind a fallback flag while the drop is being checked. A
  function the game never calls in the first place isn't Dropped: its
  status stays blank and its row says it's never called.
- **Merged**: its work is done inside a native caller's plain C++ (a
  few lines only that caller needs), so it has no function or override of
  its own in `itc_recomp/src/`. The row names the caller(s) and the
  harness that covers the work. The original is no longer called, except
  behind the caller's fallback flag.
- **Patched**: the generated code was edited in place (search for
  `PATCH:`).

A function usually moves from recompiled or Wrapped to Overridden to
Native as the `ctx` edge moves up through its callers, or straight to
Dropped when its work isn't needed.
`itc_recomp/tools/check_function_statuses.py` checks this column, in
every file of the tables, against `src/` (an override marked Native, a
status with no override, an override with no row, a row in two files);
run it after changing either, on any branch.
